\subsection{Model}\label{model}

A physical system with \(\Omega\) microstates has Boltzmann entropy
\(S = k_B \ln \Omega\). Its \textbf{information density} (negentropy) is
\(\mathcal{I} \triangleq S_{\max} - S_{\text{actual}} \geq 0\), the
amount by which the system is more ordered than thermal equilibrium. The
\textbf{accumulated negentropy} at time \(T\) is the total thermodynamic
work invested in creating and maintaining the current information
density:

\[\mathcal{N}(T) \triangleq \int_0^T \dot{W}_{\text{order}}(t) \, dt\]

where
\(\dot{W}_{\text{order}}(t) = \dot{W}_{\text{construct}}(t) + \dot{W}_{\text{maintain}}(t)\)
is the instantaneous ordering work rate (construction of new structures
plus maintenance against entropic decay). The \textbf{search cost
amplification factor} is
\(\Xi \triangleq \mathcal{N}(T) / (H_{\text{total}} \cdot k_B T \ln 2)\),
the ratio of actual accumulated negentropy to the Landauer floor.

For Earth's biosphere: \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{bio}} \sim 10^{29}\) J
(top-down solar energy estimate, cross-validated via Chaisson's energy
rate density to within a factor of 1.36),
\(H_{\text{biosphere}} \sim 10^{15}\) bits, and
\(\Xi_{\text{bio}} \approx 10^{35}\).

Under power-law rate-stock coupling
\(\dot{\mathcal{I}}_{\text{gen}} = \alpha \mathcal{N}^\beta\) with
\(\beta > 1\), the generative information rate depends superlinearly on
accumulated complexity: systems with more components have
combinatorially richer recombination spaces.

\subsection{Results}\label{results}

\textbf{Theorem 17 (Landauer's Bound).} Erasing (or creating from random
noise) one bit of information at temperature \(T\) requires minimum
energy \(E_{\text{Landauer}} = k_B T \ln 2\). Creating a system with
information density \(\mathcal{I}_{\text{bits}}\) from maximally
disordered matter requires at minimum:

\[W_{\text{create}}^{\min} = \mathcal{I}_{\text{bits}} \cdot k_B T \ln 2\]

\textbf{Theorem 18 (Minimum Replication Cost).} A system containing
\(\mathcal{I}_{\text{bits}}\) bits of functional information, where each
bit was selected from a space of \(\Omega\) equally likely candidates
and the correct configuration was found by evolutionary search over time
\(T\) with average ordering power \(\bar{P}_{\text{order}}\), requires a
minimum replication cost of:

\[W_{\text{rebuild}}^{\text{search}} \geq \mathcal{I}_{\text{bits}} \cdot k_B T \ln 2 \cdot \Xi = \mathcal{N}(T)\]

The rebuild cost is at least the accumulated negentropy itself.

\textbf{Corollary 18.1 (Replication Cost).} For Earth's biosphere:
\(W_{\text{rebuild}} \geq \mathcal{N}_{\text{bio}} \sim 10^{29}\) J.
Replicating the biosphere's accumulated negentropy requires capturing
the Sun's entire output for $\sim$4.4 minutes at 100\% efficiency (or
$\sim$3 days at 0.1\%).

\textbf{Proposition (Minimum Destruction Energy).} Dismantling a
distributed adaptive system of \(N_{\text{org}}\) organisms requires
energy \(W_{\text{destroy}} \geq \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{org}}} B_i\). For
the biosphere (\(N_{\text{org}} \sim 10^{30}\),
\(\bar{B} \sim 10^{-12}\) J): \(W_{\text{destroy}} \geq 10^{18}\) J.

\textbf{Theorem 19 (Thermodynamic Cost-Benefit Bound).} For a system
with accumulated negentropy \(\mathcal{N}\), extractable energy
\(E_{\text{destroy}}\), destruction cost \(W_{\text{destroy}}\), and
rebuild cost \(W_{\text{rebuild}} \geq \mathcal{N}\), a thermodynamic
cost-benefit gap exists when
\(E_{\text{destroy}} - W_{\text{destroy}} < W_{\text{rebuild}}\). For
Earth's biosphere:

\[\underbrace{10^{22}}_{\text{extractable energy}} - \underbrace{10^{18}}_{\text{destruction cost}} \approx 10^{22} \ll 10^{29} = \underbrace{\mathcal{N}_{\text{bio}}}_{\text{rebuild cost}}\]

The net energy yield is seven orders of magnitude smaller than the
rebuild cost.

\textbf{Corollary 19.1 (Burning-Library Inequality).}
\(E_{\text{destroy}} / \mathcal{N} = 10^{22} / 10^{29} = 10^{-7}\).
Destruction recovers one ten-millionth of the system's accumulated
investment as raw thermal energy.

\textbf{Theorem 20 (Present Value of Generative Information).} A
generative system producing novel information at sustained rate
\(\dot{\mathcal{I}}_{\text{gen}}\) bits/s, valued at \(v\)
energy-equivalents per bit of novel functional information, has present
value at discount rate \(r\):

\[PV_{\text{gen}} = \frac{\dot{\mathcal{I}}_{\text{gen}} \cdot v}{r}\]

This diverges as \(r \to 0\) (long planning horizon) and is permanently
lost upon destruction.

\textbf{Proposition (Negentropy Tipping Point).} Under the dynamics
\(d\mathcal{N}/dt = \alpha\mathcal{N}^\beta - \delta\mathcal{N} - h\)
with \(\beta > 1\):

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\alph{enumi})}
\item
  A critical threshold \(\mathcal{N}_c = (\delta/\alpha)^{1/(\beta-1)}\)
  exists below which the generative rate cannot compensate decay.
\item
  For a system at stock \(\mathcal{N}_0 > \mathcal{N}_c\), maximum
  destruction fraction avoiding collapse:
  \(\lambda^* = 1 - \mathcal{N}_c / \mathcal{N}_0\).
\item
  Below \(\mathcal{N}_c\), decline is self-reinforcing (accelerating
  decay toward informational death).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Corollary (Maximum Sustainable Extraction).} A system at stock
\(\mathcal{N}_0 > \mathcal{N}_c\) can sustain extraction of at most
\(h^*(\mathcal{N}_0) = \alpha\mathcal{N}_0^\beta - \delta\mathcal{N}_0\).
Exceeding \(h^*\) and driving \(\mathcal{N}\) below \(\mathcal{N}_c\)
triggers irreversible collapse regardless of whether extraction ceases.

\textbf{Theorem 21 (Cross-Scale Sustainability).} For a complex
dissipative structure, three independent thermodynamic bounds establish
that the costs of destruction exceed its yields:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\roman{enumi})}
\item
  \textbf{Stock bound:} \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{bio}} \sim 10^{29}\) J
  exceeds the net destruction yield \(\sim 10^{22}\) J by a factor of
  \(\sim 10^7\).
\item
  \textbf{Flow bound:} The biosphere's generative information stream has
  positive present value permanently lost upon destruction; under
  rate-stock coupling, even partial destruction degrades this stream
  superlinearly.
\item
  \textbf{Friction bound:} Dismantling a distributed adaptive biosphere
  injects thermodynamic friction into every connected network.
\end{enumerate}

Two of these three bounds (flow, friction) are independent of how the
system's complexity is valued.
