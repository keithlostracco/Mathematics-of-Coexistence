\subsection{Model}\label{model}

Agents communicate through channels modeled as binary symmetric channels
(BSC) with deception rate \(q \in [0, 0.5]\), the probability that a
given signal is strategically false. The environmental state \(\Theta\)
has entropy \(H(\Theta) = -\sum_{\theta} p(\theta) \log_2 p(\theta)\)
bits. Channel quality is measured by mutual information
\(I(\Theta; Y) = H(\Theta) - H(\Theta | Y)\), and honest communication
is defined as the regime where \(H(\Theta | Y) = 0\). Deception is the
deliberate injection of entropy: \(\Delta H = H(\Theta | Y) > 0\) when
honest transmission is achievable. The conditional entropy under BSC
deception rate \(q\) for the binary equiprobable state model
(\(\Theta \in \{0,1\}\) with equal probability) is the binary entropy
function:

\[H(\Theta | Y) = h(q) \triangleq -q \log_2 q - (1 - q) \log_2(1 - q)\]

The effective channel capacity is \(C(q) = 1 - h(q)\), which drops from
1 bit/use (honest) to 0 (total noise) as \(q\) increases from 0 to 0.5.

Agent \(B\) selects resource allocation \(x_B\) to maximize a quadratic
utility
\(U_B(x_B, \theta) = \alpha(\theta) \cdot x_B - \frac{\beta}{2} x_B^2\),
where \(\alpha(\theta) > 0\) is the state-dependent marginal yield.
Under honest communication, \(B\) achieves
\(\bar{U}_B^{\text{honest}} = \mathbb{E}[\alpha^2] / (2\beta)\).

\subsection{Results}\label{results}

\textbf{Theorem 8 (Decision Cost of Deception, Quadratic Model).} In the
quadratic utility model, the decision cost of deception is:

\[\Delta U_B = \frac{1}{2\beta}\mathbb{E}\left[\operatorname{Var}(\alpha | Y)\right] = \frac{\sigma_\alpha^2 - \sigma_{\hat{\alpha}}^2}{2\beta}\]

where \(\sigma_\alpha^2 = \operatorname{Var}(\alpha(\Theta))\) is the
prior variance and
\(\sigma_{\hat{\alpha}}^2 = \operatorname{Var}(\mathbb{E}[\alpha | Y])\)
is the explained variance. For the binary state model
(\(\alpha(\Theta) \in \{\alpha_L, \alpha_H\}\) with equal probability)
under BSC deception rate \(q\):

\[\Delta U_B(q) = \frac{q(1 - q)(\alpha_H - \alpha_L)^2}{2\beta}\]

\textbf{Theorem 9 (Metabolic Cost of Verification).} An agent receiving
information through a deceptive channel (error rate \(q > 0\)) must
expend at least:

\[W_{\text{verify}}(q) = \frac{W_{\text{honest}}}{1 - h(q)}\]

energy to achieve the same decision quality as under honest
communication. The verification overhead is:

\[\Delta W(q) = W_{\text{honest}} \cdot \frac{h(q)}{1 - h(q)}\]

This overhead is zero when \(q = 0\), grows monotonically with \(q\),
and diverges as \(q \to 0.5\).

\textbf{Corollary 9.1 (Inevitability of Deception Cost).} The minimum
cost to Agent \(B\) of deception at rate \(q > 0\) is
\(\mathcal{D}_B(q) = \min\{\Delta U_B(q), \Delta W(q)\} > 0\). There is
no costless deception.

\textbf{Theorem 10 (Cumulative Micro-Friction: Systemic Deception).} A
network of \(N\) agents with \(M\) cooperative transactions per period
and deception rate \(\bar{q} > 0\) common to all channels suffers a
total per-period efficiency loss of:

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{deception}} = M \cdot \Delta U_{\text{avg}}(\bar{q}) + M \cdot W_{\text{honest}} \cdot \frac{h(\bar{q})}{1 - h(\bar{q})}\]

This cost: (a) is zero if and only if \(\bar{q} = 0\); (b) grows
super-linearly with \(\bar{q}\) due to the
\(h(\bar{q})/(1 - h(\bar{q}))\) divergence; (c) exceeds the network's
cooperative surplus \(E_{\text{coop net}}\) at a critical deception rate
\(\bar{q}^*\), beyond which cooperation becomes energetically
unprofitable.

\textbf{Corollary 10.1 (Honesty Efficiency Principle).} Honest
communication (\(\bar{q} = 0\)) is the unique efficiency-maximizing
regime; any deviation from it imposes a strictly positive, compounding
cost on the network:

\[\phi_{\text{honest}} = 0 \iff \bar{q} = 0 \iff \text{network operates at maximum cooperative efficiency}\]

\textbf{Proposition (Serial BSC Composition).} Let information traverse
a chain of \(d\) agents, each operating a binary symmetric channel with
independent error probability \(q_l \in [0, 1/2]\). The composite
channel from the source to the receiving agent is itself a binary
symmetric channel with effective error probability:

\[q_{\text{eff}}(d; q_1, \ldots, q_d) = \frac{1 - \prod_{l=1}^{d} (1 - 2 q_l)}{2}\]

For a uniform per-agent rate \(q_l = q\),
\(q_{\text{eff}}(d, q) = (1 - (1 - 2q)^d)/2\) and end-to-end capacity
\(C_{\text{eff}}(d, q) = 1 - h(q_{\text{eff}}(d, q))\). The effective
error rate is monotonically increasing in \(d\) with
\(\lim_{d \to \infty} q_{\text{eff}}(d, q) = 1/2\) for every \(q > 0\),
and concave in \(q\) for fixed \(d \geq 2\).

\textbf{Corollary (Chain Collapse Threshold).} Fix a minimum acceptable
end-to-end channel capacity \(C_{\min} \in (0, 1)\). The maximum
tolerable per-agent corruption rate at chain length \(d\) is:

\[q^*(d; C_{\min}) = \frac{1 - (1 - 2 q_{\max})^{1/d}}{2}, \qquad q_{\max} = h^{-1}(1 - C_{\min})\]

where \(h^{-1}\) is the inverse of the binary entropy function on
\([0, 1/2]\). The threshold \(q^*(d; C_{\min})\) is monotonically
decreasing in \(d\). For \(C_{\min} = 0.05\), representative values are
\(q^*(1) \approx 0.369\), \(q^*(5) \approx 0.118\),
\(q^*(10) \approx 0.063\), \(q^*(50) \approx 0.013\).
